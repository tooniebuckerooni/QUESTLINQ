# QuestLinq — Business Plan

*Events That Bring Us Together.*

**QuestLinq.com** is a three-sided platform for live social events: **venues** find and book event **hosts**, **guests** find free and ticketed events near them, and hosts (and party-throwers everywhere) buy **printable host packs** to run great nights. Launch market: **Kingston, Ontario**, expanding to Ontario, then Canada & USA.

---

## 1. Mission & Positioning

**Mission:** connect people through play — make it easy for any venue to run a great event night, any host to get paid doing it, and any guest to find something worth leaving the house for.

**Positioning:** QuestLinq is the *marketplace and discovery layer* for live social entertainment. It is a **lateral expansion** from Fat City Entertainment (the founders' existing hosting business):

| | Fat City Entertainment | QuestLinq |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A host — runs trivia & music bingo nights | The platform — connects venues, hosts, guests |
| Revenue | Fees for hosting services | Connection fees, ticketing fees, digital products |
| Relationship | One excellent host **inside** the QuestLinq system | The system itself |

**Non-compete lines (deliberate):**
- QuestLinq does **not** sell trivia or music-bingo host kits (Fat City's core service). The Trivia/Game Show pack is on hold — see [host-packs-catalog.md](host-packs-catalog.md).
- QuestLinq **does** list Fat City's trivia nights as events (good overlap: it fills Fat City rooms and seeds QuestLinq inventory).
- Fat City's Kingston venue relationships seed QuestLinq's first host profiles, venue accounts, and case studies.

## 2. The Three Revenue Sectors

### Sector A — Host Packs (direct-to-consumer digital downloads)
Printable event toolkits sold through **LemonSqueezy** (store is live and awaiting products). Murder Mystery, Fantasy Tavern, Puzzle & Escape, Comedy Night, Theme/Retro parties, Corporate Team-Building — deliberately *not* trivia/music bingo. Full catalog, performance forecast, and pricing in [host-packs-catalog.md](host-packs-catalog.md).

- Price bands: CA$9–29 consumer, ~CA$49 corporate, bundles above that.
- Digital margin ≈ 100% after LemonSqueezy fees; bundles are pure margin expansion.
- **Strategic double-duty:** every pack contains QuestLinq-branded printable signage (QR to the site) and a "Get booked by venues — list free on QuestLinq" page. Packs are a product *and* an acquisition channel.

### Sector B — Guest Event Discovery & Ticketing
Free and ticketed event listings with heavy **"…near me" SEO** (see [seo-strategy.md](seo-strategy.md)).

- Free events: listed free, forever. They are SEO fuel, email-list builders, and the reason guests bookmark the site.
- Ticketed events: QuestLinq fee of **~5% + CA$0.79 per paid ticket** (assumption to validate; positioned under Eventbrite Canada's effective rates). Organizer can absorb or pass to buyer.
- Email capture at RSVP/checkout → weekly **"What's On in Kingston"** digest → repeat attendance.

### Sector C — Venue ↔ Host Connections (PRIORITY)
Venues browse a vetted roster of local event hosts **free**, and **pay to connect**:

- **Pay-per-connect:** ~CA$29 one-time to unlock a host's contact/booking (assumption to validate; trivial next to the value of a recurring event night).
- **Venue Pro:** ~CA$59/month — unlimited connects, "Host Wanted" gig postings, priority support.
- **Hosts never pay for leads.** Free listings, no commission on the host's booking fee, no bid-for-work. This is the anti-GigSalad/Thumbtack stance and the core of host trust.
- Later host-side upsell (only after demand is proven): featured placement.

## 3. The Flywheel

Each sector is an acquisition channel for the other two:

```
packs sold  ──►  hosts recruited (from inside the PDFs)
    │                 │
    │                 ▼
signage in venues   hosts pitch venues with their QuestLinq profile
(QR to site)          │
    │                 ▼
    │            venues pay to connect ──► event nights booked
    │                                          │
    ▼                                          ▼
guests scan/search ◄── "near me" SEO ◄── event pages created
    │
    ▼
tickets + email list + pack purchases + "I could host this" → new hosts
```

Written out: packs recruit hosts and plant branded signage inside venues → hosts use their QuestLinq profiles to pitch venues → venues pay to connect and book event nights → every event night creates a public event page → event pages win "near me" searches → guests buy tickets, join the email list, buy packs, and some become hosts.

## 4. Users & Entry Points

1. **Venue manager (the payer):** searches outcomes, not marketplaces — "how to get more customers on a Tuesday", "hire murder mystery host Kingston" — or sees a packed night at a rival venue, or gets pitched by a host. Lands on `/for-venues` or a host profile. Browses free; pays at the moment of peak intent.
2. **Guest:** searches in five intents — time-bound ("things to do in Kingston tonight"), category+local ("murder mystery near me"), occasion ("bachelorette activities Kingston" — highest ticket value), seasonal ("Halloween events Kingston"), and "free events". Also arrives via social ticket links and in-venue QR signage.
3. **Host (the supply venues pay for):** arrives via "how to host X at a bar" content, from inside a purchased pack, or after attending an event ("I could do this"). Lists free.
4. **Pack buyer:** "printable murder mystery kit" searches and Etsy/Pinterest-style discovery; may never touch the marketplace, and that's fine.

## 5. Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | Their game | QuestLinq's edge |
|---|---|---|
| **GigSalad / The Bash / Thumbtack** | Host directories; charge *performers* for leads/membership | Venues pay, hosts ride free — better supply side; event-night specialization vs "book any vendor" |
| **Eventbrite** | Ticketing at scale | Lower fees, dense *local* inventory, city-level curation Eventbrite can't do |
| **Meetup** | Interest groups | QuestLinq is venue-commercial: events at bars/breweries with hosts, not member-organized meetups |
| **Etsy printables** | Individual sellers of party kits | Cohesive branded catalog, venue-grade production quality, and a marketplace attached to every PDF |
| **Facebook Events / Kijiji** | Where venues currently hunt for hosts | Vetted profiles, media, reviews, availability — vs cold-messaging strangers |

## 7. Launch Strategy — Kingston First

**Why Kingston works:** small enough to dominate "near me" search results quickly, large enough to matter (universities, tourism, dense pub scene), and it's home turf — Fat City's venue relationships convert directly into seed inventory.

**Phase 0 — Seed (pre-launch):**
- Publish the site (GitHub + Cloudflare Pages) with the pack store live (Sector A revenue from day one).
- Hand-recruit 10–15 Kingston hosts (trivia, karaoke, comedy, mystery, paint night, drag bingo…) — free listings, personally onboarded.
- List every recurring event night in Kingston we can verify (with permission) so guest pages are never empty.

**Phase 1 — Kingston launch:**
- Turn on venue connects (manual fulfillment: an email intro is the MVP "unlock").
- 2–3 case studies with real numbers from Fat City nights ("47 tickets on a Tuesday").
- Weekly "What's On in Kingston" digest begins.

**Phase 2 — Expansion triggers.** Open a new city when Kingston shows: ≥25 host profiles, ≥10 paying venue connects/month, ≥1,000 digest subscribers, top-3 rankings on core "Kingston tonight" queries. Next: Ottawa/London/Hamilton → Toronto → rest of Canada → US border metros.

## 8. KPIs per Sector

| Sector | North star | Supporting metrics |
|---|---|---|
| A — Packs | Monthly pack revenue | Conversion rate, AOV (bundle attach), refund rate (<5%), host-recruitment clicks from PDFs |
| B — Guests | Weekly active event searchers | Organic sessions on city pages, digest subscribers, tickets sold, event-carousel impressions |
| C — Venues | Paying connects/month | Host profiles live, profile completeness, connect→booking rate, Pro subscriptions, repeat-connect rate |

## 9. Risks & Mitigations

- **Cold-start (no hosts → no venues → no hosts):** solved by hand: Fat City network seeds both sides in Kingston before anything is automated.
- **Disintermediation (venue and host cut us out after intro):** accepted at CA$29 price point — the fee is for *discovery*, not rent. Pro subscription and free event marketing keep both sides on-platform.
- **SEO takes months:** packs monetize day one; connects are sold by hand in the meantime.
- **Fat City channel conflict:** governed by the non-compete lines in §1; revisit only if Fat City itself pivots.
